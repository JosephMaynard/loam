Talk to everyone around you. No internet needed.

LOAM turns a phone, a laptop or a Raspberry Pi into a private messaging network for everyone nearby. People scan a code and they're in: no app to install, no account, no cloud.

How it works

A network of your own in three steps.

One device hosts. Everyone else just needs a phone with a camera and a browser.

  1. 1

    Start a network

    Open LOAM on an Android phone, or run npx loamnet on a laptop or Raspberry Pi. Pick a language, the kind of network you want and a name. That's the setup.

  2. 2

    People scan the code

    The host shows a code. Anyone nearby scans it with their phone's camera and LOAM opens in their browser. The phone's own hotspot works where there's no Wi-Fi at all.

  3. 3

    Everyone talks

    Channels, threads, direct messages, pictures and reactions, all on the local network. Nothing goes to the internet, even if there is some.

Get started

Host from a phone or a computer.

Both run the same network. Choose whatever you have with you.

Android phone

Runs the network, starts a hotspot and shows the codes.

  1. Download LOAM and open the file to install it. Your phone may ask you to allow installs from your browser.
  2. Answer four questions: language, kind of network, name, and how people connect.
  3. Show the code from the share screen, or turn on Display mode and leave the phone where people can see it.

Features

Everything a group needs to stay in touch.

Familiar to anyone who has used a messaging app, without the parts that need the internet.

Channels, threads and direct messages

Public channels for everyone, private ones by invitation, threaded replies that keep side conversations tidy, and one-to-one messages. Share pictures, react, search everything, and write in any of fifteen languages, left to right or right to left.

  • Pictures resized on your phone before they're sent
  • Search across every channel you can see
  • Light and dark, following your phone

Choose how private your network is

When you start a network, LOAM asks one plain question. Private and short-lived gives everyone a random name, deletes messages after an hour, lets you approve each person who joins, and leaves nothing readable once the app closes. Community lets people pick names and photos and keeps the conversation going for days.

Or choose every setting yourself: joining rules, message lifetime, roles and more.

Leave a phone in a window

Display mode fills the screen with the join codes, big enough to scan from across a room, and keeps LOAM in front. Put a spare phone on a noticeboard or a reception desk and anyone walking past can join. On a private network the code includes a fresh invite that lets people straight in, and it changes every few minutes.

A control panel in the terminal

On a laptop or Raspberry Pi, npx loamnet keeps the join code on screen and puts the rest a key away: who is connected, who has joined, the network's settings, and what went wrong if something did. Press o and LOAM opens in your browser with you as the network's admin. Messages and moderation stay in the app.

Kiosk mode locks the terminal to the join code until you enter a password, so a laptop on a front desk can welcome people without anyone changing the network from it.

Link networks together

One phone's hotspot reaches a field or a hall. For more, link another LOAM phone to the network: the host shows a single-use link code, the new phone scans it, and their public channels join up. Direct messages and private channels stay where they are.

Run it the way you want

Name the network, set who can join and how long messages last, give people moderator or greeter roles, and handle reports. Everything is in plain settings, on the host.

What about iPhone?

iPhones can join. They can't host.

Anyone with an iPhone can join a LOAM network: scan the code and it opens in Safari, with nothing to install. Hosting one is different, because iOS doesn't let an app do two things a host needs.

  1. An app can't start a hotspot

    A LOAM phone makes its own Wi-Fi, so people can join anywhere, with no router. On an iPhone, only you can turn on Personal Hotspot, in Settings, and it needs a mobile plan that allows it.

  2. Apps pause in the background

    iOS pauses an app soon after you switch away from it or lock the screen, and an app can't keep a server running. A network hosted on an iPhone would stop the moment its screen went dark.

  3. Got a Mac? Host from that

    npx loamnet runs on macOS, as well as Linux and Windows. One command and your Mac is the host, with iPhones and everything else joining from the browser.

Privacy and safety

Private because there's nothing to collect.

LOAM has no accounts, no cloud and no company in the middle. What people post stays on the device that runs the network, and on their own phones.

Read the privacy policy
  • No sign-up No email, no phone number. Everyone gets a generated name and picture, or picks their own.
  • Encrypted connections Joining by the code encrypts messages between your phone and the host, so nobody else on the Wi-Fi can read them. Pictures and files are encrypted too on a network that requires it.
  • Encrypted storage Messages can be stored encrypted on the host, with a key that never leaves the phone.
  • Emergency reset Press and hold to erase the whole network at once, on the host and on every phone connected to it. LOAM then closes, ready to start again.
  • No tracking in the app No analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting. Nothing phones home, so if something goes wrong, please open an issue or email us.
